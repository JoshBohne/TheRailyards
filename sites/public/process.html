Build log

From three renderings
to a place you can enter.

Astra built the first reproducible Blender world from public concept images. Fable inherited the scene, challenged the parts that looked convincing but were spatially wrong, and turned the corrected model into rendered routes and an interactive browser replay.

This page documents the method, the handoffs, the failures, and what the numbers actually mean.

Division of labor

Two agents.
Different jobs.

The useful story is not that an agent pressed a Blender button. It is that one agent built a documented system and another could audit, challenge, and extend it without starting over.

A

GPT-6 Astra · Codex

Construct the world.

Astra inventoried the source images, solved the fixed cameras, established scale, generated the stadium and district through Python, coordinated component workers, and wrote a handoff that named its own unresolved problems.

  • Reference inventory and pixel controls
  • Camera calibration and site registration
  • Blender generators for bowl, field, facade, park, river, and city
  • Repeatable builds, renders, and a defect handoff
F

Claude Fable 5.1 · Claude Code

Interrogate the result.

Fable reopened the actual scene, rendered diagnostic views, proved where geometry and geography disagreed, corrected the generators, expanded the skyline and concourses, created the guided films, and delivered the browser experiences.

  • Scoreboard, support, lake, and skyline corrections
  • Same-camera before-and-after evidence
  • Arrival, terrace, river, and night experience films
  • Shared-clock 3D replay and seat-level views

The loop

A reconstruction pipeline,
not a one-shot prompt.

The scene improved by cycling between evidence, code, geometry, and rendered output. When the render exposed a defect, the fix went back into the generator rather than remaining a fragile manual edit.

  1. 01Read

    Inventory published imagery, maps, and known dimensions.

  2. 02Calibrate

    Fit fixed cameras and a shared coordinate frame.

  3. 03Generate

    Build the scene from versioned Python modules.

  4. 04Render

    Inspect the same views people will actually see.

  5. 05Challenge

    Measure clearances, projections, bearings, and failures.

  6. 06Preserve

    Push accepted corrections back into reproducible code.

sources→scene-spec.json→build_blockout.py→build_detail.py→render→browser

Verification

The failures were more useful than the beauty renders.

A reconstruction can look plausible while still being wrong. The project used plans, sections, fixed-camera comparisons, geographic maps, and browser tests to find defects that an aerial hero image could hide.

Geometry

The scoreboard was inside the field.

A point-in-polygon check put its center 5.2 meters inside the modeled boundary. The complete board and support footprints were re-triangulated from the source cameras and moved behind the wall.

Structure

The right-field bowl ended in midair.

Low exterior views and a section exposed the gap. A podium, river-facing arcade, pylons, and a tower link gave the visible decks a continuous support path.

Geography

Chicago ended in pavement, not Lake Michigan.

The original background plane continued east across the shoreline. Geographic data replaced it with separate land, river, harbor, and lake geometry.

Interface

The first replay hid the feature people wanted.

Browser review showed that the seat picker needed to be visible immediately, the boat camera needed a clearer splash, and the arrival path should stop before an empty endpoint.

Limits

What the project still does not know.

The public imagery does not resolve every level, support, circulation path, material, or dimension. The browser export also simplifies materials, trees, crowds, and geometry for performance. The replay’s players, ball flight, camera paths, and splash are authored illustrations.

No amount of token usage converts those unknowns into approved architecture. The honest output is a navigable interpretation with explicit uncertainty, not a digital twin.
