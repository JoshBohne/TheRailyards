    1. From rail yard to ballpark

      How the White Sox could end up on the river, in five steps.

      ↓
    2. February 2024Earlier plan

      The Sox pitch The 78

      Related Midwest and the White Sox propose a ballpark on the 62 acres across the river, with a $1.2 billion public ask. It stalls, and the Chicago Fire take the site.

      MLB.com · Feb 2024
    3. March 3, 2026Under construction

      McDonald’s Park breaks ground

      The Fire’s privately funded 22,000-seat stadium starts rising at The 78. Target opening: 2028. The ballpark would face it across the river.

      Chicago Fire · Mar 3, 2026
    4. August 14, 2026Existing

      Amtrak wins the money to move

      Federal grants of up to $572 million for a new maintenance yard, plus $125 million from Justin Ishbia’s Canal Edge. This 47-acre yard south of Roosevelt is what would be freed up.

      Amtrak · Aug 14, 2026
    5. Fall 2026 → 2028Proposed

      The new yard goes to Bridgeport

      The rail yard is already there. Union Pacific’s Canal Street yard has sat beside Rate Field for over a century, idle since 2019. Amtrak would store trains at the north end and build the shop at the south end, between 33rd and 35th. No new corridor, no new footprint. Residents and Ald. Nicole Lee oppose it; Amtrak says passenger trains, fewer diesel moves, LED lighting.

      Block Club · Sept 9, 2026
    6. September 5, 2026Proposed

      The Railyards is unveiled

      Canal Edge shows AECOM renderings of a brick riverfront ballpark facing McDonald’s Park. The White Sox begin a multi-month study. Their Rate Field lease runs through 2029; nothing is approved or financed yet.

      Block Club · Sept 6, 2026
