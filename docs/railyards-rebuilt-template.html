The Railyards · Chicago White Sox · AECOM concept, Sept 2026

Railyards,
Rebuilt

Three published concept renderings, one site plan, and two AI coding agents turned into an editable 3-D model of the proposed White Sox ballpark on the South Branch, with the real Chicago skyline and Lake Michigan where they actually are.

Made over one weekend by Josh Bohne with OpenAI Codex (GPT-6 Astra) and Claude Code (Claude Fable 5.1). Every image below is a native Blender render of the model, not the AECOM artwork, unless labelled.

How it was made

Method

Sources, not vibes

The model is fitted to three published AECOM images (north aerial, south aerial, the view from the Roosevelt bridge) and the site plan. A 90-foot base path is the scale prior. Three perspective cameras were solved against pixel controls; everything traced from a picture records which image and which pixels it came from.

Geography is real: OpenStreetMap footprints for 780 existing buildings and 238 more in the near South Loop, the USGS shoreline for the lake, published heights for the landmarks. Buildings are never moved to look better from a seat; the cameras go to the buildings.

Everything is a script

The scene is generated by about forty Python modules run through the Blender command line — bowl, envelope, seats, boards, park, bridges, skyline, lake. Live edits in Blender were pushed back into the generators, so a clean rebuild reproduces the file. A rebuild takes about a minute; the eight-view render pass about ten.

  • build_blockout.py → build_detail.py → finalize_scene.py then run_v4_renders.sh
  • 1,582 objects, 1.79 M mesh vertices, 26,950 instanced seats, 21,282 spectators, 290 trees
  • Blender 5.2.1 LTS, Cycles on Apple Metal

Two agents, two jobs

Astra (Codex) did the long build on Saturday: source inventories, camera calibration, the bowl and district, thirty-five generators, and a written handoff listing what it believed was wrong. That handoff was honest — it flagged the scoreboard and the lake itself.

Fable 5.1 (Claude Code) took the handoff on Sunday. It rendered diagnostic cameras before touching anything, triangulated the board from all three source views instead of guessing, found that the skyline data and the city data disagreed by 33 m and fixed the registration once, fetched the lake from USGS when the city's GIS server timed out, and ranked candidate towers by how many pixels they would actually occupy from the seats. A reviewer model checked the plan before the build and the result before sign-off.

Verification

Every fix has a before/after from the same camera, an orthographic plan with clearances in metres, and the model's own geometry projected back into the AECOM photos. The V3 baseline is preserved and hash-checked. The ledger says what is resolved, what is uncertain, and what was deliberately left.

What this is not

It is a conceptual reconstruction of concept art, not a surveyed building. Outfield distances, hidden structure, materials and the future towers across the river are inferred. The scoreboard graphics and players are placeholders. AECOM's own disclaimer applies twice over: design, uses and locations remain subject to diligence, engineering and approvals.
